# Survey on World Models in Artificial Intelligence: From Latent Dynamics to Generative Interactive Simulators

## TL;DR
- World models serve as internal predictive representations of environmental dynamics, enabling model-based reinforcement learning, planning, and offline imagination [1][2].
- Foundational architectures combined compact spatial compression (VAEs) with recurrent state-space models (RSSM) to enable agents to learn policies entirely in imagined latent spaces [3][4][5].
- Recent advances replace discrete token models with video-generative diffusion transformers (CDiT) and autoregressive denoising to support fine-grained action conditioning and interactive simulation at scale [6][7][8][9].
- Embodied frameworks like DreamerV3 and DayDreamer achieve sample-efficient online adaptation across physical robots, game environments, and diverse control tasks [2][10].
- Evaluation benchmarks such as WorldArena highlight a persistent perception-functionality gap, revealing that high visual generation fidelity does not automatically ensure robust closed-loop policy execution [11].

## Background
World models in artificial intelligence refer to computational systems that learn internal predictive representations of how the environment evolves over time in response to agent actions [1][2]. Inspired by cognitive science, where biological agents maintain internal mental models of physical reality to anticipate outcomes without direct trial-and-error, machine learning approaches formulate world models to predict future observations, rewards, and state transitions [1][2]. In reinforcement learning and robotics, model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) contrasts with model-free paradigms by leveraging these learned transition dynamics to plan actions, generate synthetic experience, and dramatically improve sample efficiency [1][2].

Early milestones established the tripartite Vision-Memory-Controller (V-M-C) structure, where convolutional encoders compressed high-dimensional frames into latent vectors, recurrent neural networks (RNNs or LSTMs) modeled temporal latent dynamics, and controllers evolved policies within hallucinated dreams [3]. Subsequent breakthroughs introduced Recurrent State-Space Models (RSSMs) to handle both stochastic and deterministic dynamics in latent space, underpinning scalable frameworks like PlaNet and Dreamer [4][5]. Over the past few years, the field has transitioned from low-dimensional latent models to large-scale video-generative world models powered by diffusion transformers and autoregressive architectures, bridging perception, language conditioning, and robotic control [7][12][9].

## Foundational Architectures and Latent Dynamics
Early computational formulations of world models demonstrated that agents could learn effective policies by operating inside simulated mental representations rather than raw pixel environments. Ha and Schmidhuber introduced a recurrent world model comprising a Vision (V) model using a Convolutional Variational Autoencoder to compress frames into latent vectors $z_t$, a Memory (M) model combining an LSTM with a Mixture Density Network (MDN-RNN) to predict future latents, and a Controller (C) model that mapped latent representations to actions [3]. Evaluated on the Car Racing task, the agent achieved high performance and proved capable of learning policies entirely within its hallucinated dream environment [3].

To address partial observability and stochasticity in continuous control, Hafner et al. developed PlaNet (Deep Planning Network), which introduced the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) [4]. The RSSM couples deterministic recurrent transitions with stochastic distributions, enabling online planning via the cross-entropy method in latent space while requiring approximately 200 times fewer episodes than strong model-free baselines [4]. Building upon the RSSM, the Dreamer family of agents demonstrated that policies and value functions could be learned purely through multi-step latent imagination by propagating analytic gradients of state values back through imagined trajectories via backpropagation through time [5]. DreamerV3 further unified these principles across over 150 diverse tasks with a single set of fixed hyperparameters, successfully mastering robot locomotion, Atari games, and collecting diamonds from scratch in Minecraft without human data [2].

## Video-Generative and Diffusion-Based World Models
As generative modeling advanced, world models expanded from compact low-dimensional latent spaces to full-scale video generation models capable of synthesizing high-fidelity future visual frames conditioned on past observations and actions [6][7]. Diffusion models and Conditional Diffusion Transformers (CDiT) have emerged as powerful backbones for visual world modeling. For instance, DIAMOND demonstrated that continuous diffusion models capture fine-grained visual details in Atari environments that are crucial for robust reinforcement learning and policy training [8]. Similarly, the Navigation World Model (NWM) scaled up to one billion parameters to predict egocentric navigation trajectories using diffusion transformers trained on diverse human and robotic video collections [6].

Recent 2024–2025 architectures incorporate precise action-conditioning modules to align physical interactions with generated video streams. IRASim introduced a fine-grained robot manipulation world model utilizing a diffusion transformer architecture with frame-level action conditioning embedded within each transformer block, enabling autoregressive rollouts over 150+ frames and improving diffusion policy IoU on Push-T from 0.637 to 0.961 [7][13]. Other recent models, such as Astra with autoregressive denoising [9], Yume with masked video diffusion transformers [14], GameGen-X for open-world interactive game generation [15], and Qwen-RobotWorld for language-conditioned embodied video generation [12], reflect a broad convergence toward general interactive simulation engines capable of open-ended prediction and multi-modal grounding.

## Embodied AI, Robotics, and Physical Deployment
The integration of world models into robotics and embodied AI bridges the gap between simulated prediction and physical real-world deployment. DayDreamer applied the Dreamer world model algorithm online directly to four physical robots without simulators, enabling a quadruped robot to learn to roll over, stand up, and walk from scratch in one hour, and adapt to physical pushes within ten minutes [10]. Across robotic arms and mobile platforms, DayDreamer demonstrated that learned internal dynamics allow physical agents to acquire complex manipulation and navigation skills efficiently from camera feeds and sparse rewards [10].

Surveys on world models for robot learning categorize these systems into diverse paradigms, including inverse dynamics model (IDM) policies, single-backbone predictive models, mixture-of-experts architectures, and unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models [1]. These models serve a dual purpose: acting as generative simulators for offline policy training and data engines, and functioning as online mental models for model-based planning [1]. However, deploying world models in real-world environments entails challenges such as representation drift, computational latency, and simulation-to-reality gaps when uncalibrated visual artifacts accumulate during long-horizon autoregressive rollouts [1][11].

## Benchmarks, Evaluation, and the Perception-Functionality Gap
Evaluating world models has historically relied on task-specific reward accumulation or qualitative visual inspection. To address this, recent efforts have introduced unified evaluation frameworks like WorldArena, which systematically assesses embodied world models across 16 perception metrics, embodied task functionality (data engines, policy evaluators, and action planners), and human evaluations [11]. WorldArena introduces the EWMScore index to capture multi-dimensional performance, demonstrating strong alignment with human judgment [11].

Crucially, evaluations across representative models on datasets such as RobotTwin 2.0 uncovered a significant "perception-functionality gap" [11]. High visual generation fidelity and aesthetic realism do not automatically translate into robust closed-loop action planning or effective embodied task execution [11]. This finding indicates that future research must prioritize action-grounded dynamics and functional utility over pure visual realism, ensuring that world models accurately capture physical constraints, causal interactions, and consequence-aware state transitions necessary for reliable autonomous deployment [11].

## Trends and open problems
The field of world models has shifted from specialized low-dimensional latent recurrent networks to massive, multi-modal video-generative transformers capable of interactive simulation. Key emerging trends include the scaling of diffusion and autoregressive architectures to billions of parameters, language-conditioned embodiment, and cross-domain zero-shot generalization [6][12][9].

Despite these advances, several fundamental open problems remain:
- **The Perception-Functionality Gap:** As demonstrated by benchmarks like WorldArena, high-fidelity video generation does not guarantee effective downstream action planning or robust physical control [11].
- **Compounding Error in Long-Horizon Rollouts:** Autoregressive generation over extended time horizons frequently suffers from drift, blurriness, and unphysical artifacts that degrade policy evaluation [1][11].
- **Computational Efficiency and Latency:** High-resolution diffusion-based world models are computationally intensive and far from real-time execution, necessitating distillation and hardware acceleration [6][7].
- **Causal Robustness:** Ensuring that learned world models capture true physical invariants rather than superficial statistical correlations remains a central challenge for safe embodied deployment [1][11].
