# Survey on Efficient Inference and Small Language Models

## TL;DR
- Efficient inference for large and small language models relies on memory-aware attention algorithms and virtual memory paging such as FlashAttention and PagedAttention to eliminate major memory bottlenecks, reduce HBM reads and writes, and optimize KV cache utilization without altering the quadratic arithmetic complexity of dense self-attention [1][2][3].
- Post-training quantization and compression methods like GPTQ, AWQ, and gradient compensation balance footprint and accuracy without requiring full model retraining [4][5][6].
- Compact models and specialized small language models leverage distillation, optimized training recipes, and hardware co-design to achieve high throughput on edge devices and standard hardware [7][8][9].
- Advanced serving and decoding techniques—including speculative sampling, sparse-first engines, and token-level routing—maximize concurrency and hardware utilization across heterogeneous workloads [10][11][12].

## Background
The rapid adoption of transformer-based language models across diverse domains has created an urgent demand for efficient inference methodologies. Because self-attention exhibits quadratic time and memory complexity with respect to sequence length, processing long contexts and high-throughput concurrent requests places severe strain on GPU memory and compute resources [1][2]. Foundational developments in efficient transformer design established that hardware-agnostic theoretical improvements are often insufficient without considering input-output (IO) awareness and memory hierarchies [1]. Consequently, modern inference frameworks must jointly optimize numerical representation, memory layout, decoding schedules, and routing architectures to overcome memory bandwidth bottlenecks and latency constraints [2][3]. Concurrently, the development of compact and small language models (SLMs) provides an alternative pathway, enabling high performance on resource-constrained edge hardware through targeted pre-training and distillation [7][8].

## Attention Mechanisms and Memory Management
Transformer inference throughput is frequently bounded by memory capacity and bandwidth rather than raw arithmetic compute, primarily due to the dynamic growth of the key-value (KV) cache during autoregressive generation. FlashAttention demonstrated that accounting for reads and writes between levels of GPU memory through IO-aware tiling achieves exact self-attention computation with significant wall-clock speedups [1]. Note that the speedups reported in FlashAttention benchmarks correspond primarily to training regimes [1], and inference speedups vary based on specific hardware, batch sizes, sequence lengths, and baselines. Building upon memory-efficient attention primitives, PagedAttention adapts virtual memory paging techniques from operating systems to partition the KV cache into fixed-size blocks, avoiding external fragmentation, bounding last-block internal waste, and supporting dynamic memory sharing [2][3]. Subsequent systems extend these principles to address emerging architectural complexities; for instance, Jenga provides specialized memory allocation for heterogeneous embedding dimensions and access patterns in modern LLM architectures [13], while SparseEngine coordinates KV-cache lifecycles across sparse-first execution workflows [10].

## Quantization and Weight Compression
To reduce memory footprint and accelerate inference without expensive full-model retraining, post-training quantization (PTQ) has become a critical pillar of deployment [4][6]. GPTQ introduced a one-shot weight quantization method using approximate second-order Hessian information to compress large generative models with minimal accuracy loss [4]. To preserve generalization across domains and accommodate activation distributions, Activation-aware Weight Quantization (AWQ) targets salient weights based on their corresponding activation magnitudes [5]. Further advances address limitations in standard layer-wise objectives and fixed Hessian estimates; for example, Generalized Gradient Compensation ($G^2PTQ$) provides unified gradient-based correction for post-training quantization [6], while related methods explore gradient-based mixed-precision allocation and joint activation-aware pruning [14][15]. On edge hardware, these compression techniques are combined with hybrid execution pipelines to enable real-time deployment of models such as Qwen2.5 [9].

## Small Language Models and Distillation
While massive models dominate general capabilities, small language models and task-specific distilled architectures offer compelling advantages in latency, cost, and on-device deployability [7][8]. Knowledge distillation, pioneered by encoder models like DistilBERT, enables compact student models to retain a large fraction of teacher representation capabilities during pre-training [7], a paradigm extended by specialized CPU runtimes and anomaly detection pipelines [16][17]. Modern open-source small language models, such as TinyLlama, provide a pretrained 1.1B autoregressive decoder-only architecture that leverages carefully curated training datasets and efficient attention primitives to achieve strong downstream performance [8]. Furthermore, frameworks like TinyLLaVA and TinyLLaVA Factory apply modular design principles to small-scale multimodal models, ensuring extensibility, reproducibility, and efficient execution on resource-constrained hardware [18][19].

## Decoding Acceleration and Serving Systems
Autoregressive generation can be memory-bandwidth limited depending on batch size, sequence length, and hardware characteristics. Speculative sampling and decoding alleviate this bottleneck by using a lightweight draft model to generate a candidate token sequence autoregressively, while a large target model evaluates those candidate positions in parallel in a single forward pass [20][12]. Algorithmic speculative decoding guarantees sampling from the exact target distribution via specialized acceptance and rejection criteria, whereas approximate sparse or quantized acceleration methods trade off exact distribution matching for higher speedups [20][12][21]. To integrate sparse attention with speculative execution, SpecAttn leverages draft-model attention weights to identify important target tokens, eliminating redundant computations [21]. At the infrastructure level, modern serving engines such as vLLM combine continuous batching, chunked prefill, and multi-backend quantization support [3], while advanced token-level routers like TokenRouter distribute inference workloads across models to optimize the cost-quality Pareto frontier [11].

## Trends and open problems
Recent developments highlight a transition toward heterogeneous, sparse-first, and multi-model inference pipelines that tightly couple hardware primitives with algorithmic design [13][10][11]. However, significant open problems remain. Maintaining numerical stability and zero degradation under extreme low-bit quantization (such as 2-bit or binary representations) continues to challenge post-training methods [4][5][6]. Moreover, as context lengths extend to hundreds of thousands of tokens, managing sparse KV-cache layouts without introducing synchronization overhead or hardware fragmentation requires more flexible lifecycle contracts [10]. Finally, balancing dynamic token-level routing with continuous batching and prefix caching in production serving systems introduces intricate scheduling trade-offs that demand further investigation [11][3].
