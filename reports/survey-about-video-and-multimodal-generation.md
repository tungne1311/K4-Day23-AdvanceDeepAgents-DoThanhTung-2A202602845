# A Survey of Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Unification, and Open-Source Systems

## TL;DR
- Video generation has shifted from per-frame image techniques to scalable latent diffusion models and diffusion transformers processing spacetime latent patches [1][2].
- Multimodal foundation models integrate video understanding and any-to-any generation by aligning visual modalities into shared language or discrete token spaces, with models like Video-LLaVA focusing on visual understanding and NExT-GPT and VideoPoet delivering multimodal generation [3][4][5].
- CogVideoX employs a 3D VAE and an expert transformer with expert adaptive LayerNorm to generate 10-second continuous videos [6].
- Open-Sora 2.0 utilizes full attention and an efficient multi-stage training pipeline leveraging pre-trained open image backbones for a reported training cost of $200k [7].
- Advanced closed-loop world models like WorldGuide formulate procedural video generation as goal-directed task execution by coupling context planners with action-realizing video executors [8].

## Background
Sora explores large-scale training of generative models by jointly training text-conditional diffusion models on videos and images of variable durations, resolutions, and aspect ratios using a transformer architecture operating on compressed spacetime latent patches [1]. Runway Gen-2 evolved from latent diffusion approaches to tackle text-guided video generation directly, operating as a model that can take any starting image and predict its motion [2].

## Video Generation Architectures and Latent Diffusion Models
Modern video generation models build heavily upon latent diffusion and transformer backbones. Sora pioneered the use of text-conditional diffusion models trained jointly on videos and images of variable durations, resolutions, and aspect ratios [1]. By operating on spacetime patches of video and image latent codes, Sora achieves high-fidelity generation up to a full minute in duration [1]. To achieve temporal consistency and efficient scaling, video diffusion models adopt specialized architectures. CogVideoX utilizes a 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and an expert transformer architecture incorporating expert adaptive LayerNorm to generate 10-second continuous videos at 16 fps [6]. Similarly, Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) scales latent video diffusion models by inserting temporal convolution and attention layers into latent diffusion backbones, supported by curated video datasets and micro-conditioning on frame rates [9]. These architectural enhancements allow diffusion models to capture motion priors across varied dataset distributions while maintaining high spatial fidelity.

## Unified Multimodal Models and Any-to-Any Generation
Bridging visual understanding and generation requires tight integration between text, image, video, and audio modalities. Video-LLaVA addresses the "misalignment before projection" issue in large vision-language models by pre-binding raw images and videos directly into the language feature space using LanguageBind encoders before projection [3]. This enables the Vicuna-7B LLM backbone to learn effectively from interleaved image-video datasets in a single training epoch [3], functioning strictly as a visual understanding model rather than a content generator. For any-to-any generation, NExT-GPT integrates an LLM with multimodal adaptors and distinct diffusion decoders (such as Stable Diffusion for images, Zeroscope for video, and AudioLDM for audio), tuning only a small fraction (~1%) of certain projection layers to enable cross-modal semantic reasoning and generation across arbitrary input-output combinations [4]. Alternatively, VideoPoet casts zero-shot video and audio generation entirely as a decoder-only autoregressive multimodal framework utilizing a transformer architecture over diverse conditioning signals [5].

## Open-Source Ecosystems and Training Cost Efficiency
The democratization of video generation relies heavily on open-source ecosystems that reduce the prohibitive costs of training foundational models from scratch. Open-Sora 2.0 demonstrates that commercial-level video generation can be achieved at a reported training cost of $200k by initializing from FLUX—an open text-to-image model—and employing a three-stage pipeline (low-resolution T2V, low-resolution I2V, and high-resolution I2V fine-tuning) utilizing deep compression video VAEs (Video DC-AE) [7]. While open-source latent diffusion models focus on high-resolution open-loop synthesis [7], recent closed-loop world models like WorldGuide introduce goal-directed frameworks that integrate planning and execution for procedural video generation [8].

## Comparative Analysis of Video and Multimodal Approaches
Different paradigms offer distinct trade-offs between architectural complexity, modality unification, and computational overhead. Spacetime patch diffusion models (e.g., Sora [1]) achieve exceptional visual realism and temporal length but lack fully public architectural specifications. Latent video diffusion frameworks (e.g., CogVideoX [6], SVD [9], Open-Sora [7]) provide transparent architectures and high-resolution outputs through specialized 3D VAEs and transformer backbones, with Open-Sora initializing from FLUX [7] and CogVideoX utilizing expert adaptive LayerNorm [6]. Multimodal LVLMs and token-based frameworks (e.g., Video-LLaVA [3], NExT-GPT [4], VideoPoet [5]) successfully unify understanding and generation, with Video-LLaVA specializing in visual comprehension [3], NExT-GPT connecting pretrained modality encoders and decoders with an LLM [4], and VideoPoet delivering decoder-only multimodal generation [5]. Furthermore, while standard video generation models primarily perform open-loop rollout generation, goal-directed architectures like WorldGuide [8] implement closed-loop task execution to adapt generation based on intermediate visual states.

## Trends and open problems
Current developments highlight three primary directions: Open-Sora focuses on efficiency and reproducibility with a reported $200k training cost utilizing pre-trained image models and multi-stage pipelines [7]; CogVideoX emphasizes robust text-video alignment and temporal coherence via specialized transformer architectures [6]; and WorldGuide addresses the transition from open-loop to closed-loop procedural execution, managing long-horizon state tracking and action-realizing executors [8].
